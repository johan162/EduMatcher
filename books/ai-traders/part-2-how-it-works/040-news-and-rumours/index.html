    News and Rumours¶

    Learning objectives

    After reading this chapter you will be able to:

    • Read a headline the way the simulation means it: scope, kind, sentiment and the secret impact behind it
    • Follow a rumour from its first headline to its confirmation or retraction
    • Predict how the AI traders and the market maker react to news
    • Turn generated news up, down or off, and publish news of your own in front of a class

    Prerequisites: The True Value.

    Why news¶

    In the simulation, as on a real exchange, the true value of a share does two things: it drifts a little all the time, and now and then it jumps because something happened. The jumps are what news is for. A headline tells the market that the value has moved — and roughly which way — before the price has caught up. Who reads it first, who believes it and who ignores it is what turns a headline into a price move you can watch.

    Anatomy of a headline¶

    Field Public? Meaning
    Scope and targets yes One symbol, one sector, or the whole market
    Kind yes EARNINGS, GUIDANCE, MNA, REGULATORY, PRODUCT, LEGAL, MANAGEMENT or MACRO
    Headline yes The text, e.g. "NVDA raises its outlook"
    Sentiment yes How good it sounds, from −1 (very bad) to +1 (very good)
    Status yes CONFIRMED (it happened), RUMOUR, then CONFIRMED or RETRACTED
    Credibility yes, rumours only How believable the rumour looks, 0 to 1
    Impact no The log move it causes in the true value: 0.05 is about +5.1 %, −0.10 about −9.5 %

    Students see everything but the impact: in terminal-gui's news view, the API (GET /api/v1/news) and pm-ticker. The impact is the market model's secret, exactly like the true value.

    How far a headline moves values. A symbol headline moves that symbol by its impact. A sector headline moves each symbol of the sector by beta_sector × impact, a market headline every symbol by beta_market × impact — so with the s300-sim betas a sector headline moves its symbols by about 35–60 % of the impact, and a market headline every symbol by 35–60 % of it.

    Generated news¶

    Left alone, pm-market-sim writes news itself, at random moments during continuous trading. The s300-sim example keeps the defaults:

    news:
  rate_symbol: 6.0         # symbol headlines per trading day, whole exchange
  rate_sector: 0.5         # sector headlines per trading day
  rate_market: 0.3         # market headlines per trading day
  rumour_share: 0.3        # share that starts as a rumour
  rumour_confirm: 0.6      # chance a rumour is confirmed rather than retracted
  rumour_delay_min: 0.02   # trading days before a rumour resolves (uniform)
  rumour_delay_max: 0.15

    What that means on a 300-symbol exchange:

    On average
    Symbol headlines 6 a day — one every hour or so; any one share gets news about once every 50 trading days
    Sector headlines one every two trading days
    Market headlines one every three or four trading days
    Rumours about 2 a day; they resolve 8 to 60 minutes of continuous trading later, 3 in 5 confirmed

    A "trading day" here is the 6.5 hours of continuous trading; the rates are per trading day whatever speed the calendar runs at (see Accelerated Time).

    What kind of news, how big¶

    Each scope draws a kind, and each kind has its own size: the impact is normally distributed with the kind's mean and spread.

    Kind Scopes Share of symbol news Impact mean Impact spread (σ) Typical move
    EARNINGS symbol 35 % 0 5 % ±5 %
    GUIDANCE symbol 17 % 0 3 % ±3 %
    PRODUCT symbol 17 % +0.5 % 2 % ±2 %
    REGULATORY symbol, sector 9 % −1 % 4 % ±4 %, a little more often bad
    LEGAL symbol 9 % −1 % 3 % ±3 %, a little more often bad
    MANAGEMENT symbol 9 % 0 2 % ±2 %
    MNA symbol 4 % +6 % 6 % a takeover bid: mostly up, sometimes a lot
    MACRO sector, market — 0 1.5 % ±1.5 % before the betas

    Sector headlines are REGULATORY or MACRO (half each); market headlines are always MACRO.

    Sentiment: a hint, not the answer¶

    The sentiment students see is the impact made public, but blurred: roughly impact ÷ (2 × the kind's spread), plus a little noise (σ 0.15), clipped to ±1. So an earnings beat of one spread (+5 %) reads about +0.5, a big miss of two spreads (−10 %) about −1. The sign is almost always right; the size is only a guide. Two headlines with the same sentiment can move the value by quite different amounts — which is why value traders, who see the value itself, keep correcting what news traders start.

    Rumours¶

    stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> RUMOUR: headline with a credibility\nvalue does not move
    RUMOUR --> CONFIRMED: confirmed\nvalue moves by the impact
    RUMOUR --> RETRACTED: retracted\nvalue never moves
    CONFIRMED --> [*]
    RETRACTED --> [*]

    A rumour is a headline that may not be true. It carries a credibility and moves nothing in the model. Later it is confirmed — a second headline, and only then does the value move — or retracted, and the value never moves at all. Prices often do move on a rumour, though: that is traders betting on it, and when a retracted rumour's price move unwinds, someone has lost money.

    Credibility is informative but not reliable. A generated rumour that will be confirmed gets a credibility between 0.4 and 0.9; one that will be retracted between 0.1 and 0.6. A credibility of 0.8 is almost surely true, one of 0.2 almost surely false, and anything in 0.4–0.6 is a coin toss.

    Who reacts, and how¶

    Who Reacts to How
    news-trader (5 agents) every headline touching its symbols Hears it after a lag of its own (median 5 s), then trades in the headline's direction while its conviction — |sentiment|, times credibility for a rumour, halving every 2 minutes — stays above 0.15; pays up to 0.08 × conviction beyond the price it first saw
    value-investor, institutional (30 agents) the true value, which news moves Buy or sell toward the new value as soon as it moves. On a rumour, which does not move the value, they shift their own view by 0.05 × sentiment × credibility
    MM01, the market maker the true value Moves its own price 30 % of the way toward the value every second and re-quotes once that has moved 15 ticks — so a confirmed headline moves its quotes within seconds, and a rumour does not move them at all
    Everyone else (65 agents) prices only Trend followers and scalpers chase the move once it shows; contrarians fade it

    Put together, a confirmed headline is priced in fast: in a test with an anchored market maker and the same kinds of trader on 150 symbols, a +10 % earnings surprise moved the price +6.5 % within 5 seconds and +10 % within 15 seconds. Rumours leave much less trace than you might expect. The market maker sells to every rumour buyer at its value-anchored price, so in the same test a credible good rumour (sentiment 0.7, credibility 0.5) lifted the price under 3 %, and a sector rumour moved its sector by less than 1 %. To show students how a rumour moves a market, run without the market maker — see Tuning the Traders.

    Steering the news¶

    Fewer, more, or none¶

    Change the rates in market_sim.yaml (how to deploy your copy: Tuning the Traders):

    You want Set
    A quiet market where you alone publish news every rate_* to 0
    A news-heavy market, a headline every few minutes rate_symbol: 60 (and perhaps rate_sector: 3)
    More rumours, harder to read rumour_share: 0.6, rumour_confirm: 0.5
    Rumours that linger for a lesson rumour_delay_min: 0.1, rumour_delay_max: 0.3
    No market-wide shocks rate_market: 0

    Each rate is a Poisson rate: the gaps between headlines are random, so rate_symbol: 60 gives ten an hour on average, sometimes three in a minute, sometimes none for twenty.

    Publishing news yourself¶

    pm-news sends a headline to the running market model as an ADMIN participant. In the container:

    docker exec edumatcher-sim pm-news --id OPS01 inject --symbol NVDA \
    --kind EARNINGS --sentiment 0.8 --impact 0.08 \
    --headline "NVDA smashes estimates on data-centre demand"

    The value of NVDA moves +8.3 % at once; the price follows within seconds. A rumour you inject waits for you:

    docker exec edumatcher-sim pm-news --id OPS01 inject --sector ENERGY \
    --kind REGULATORY --sentiment -0.7 --impact -0.06 --rumour --credibility 0.6
N12
docker exec edumatcher-sim pm-news --id OPS01 confirm N12     # or: retract N12

    inject prints the headline's id (N12 here). A rumour you inject never resolves on its own. Your headlines and generated ones mix freely; set the rates to 0 if yours should be the only ones.

    Scenarios¶

    For a lesson, script the news in a file and play it:

    docker exec edumatcher-sim pm-news --id OPS01 play \
    /opt/edumatcher/.venv/lib/python3.13/site-packages/docs/examples/news/earnings-surprise.yaml

    Three scenarios ship with EduMatcher — earnings-surprise, rumour-confirmed and rumour-retracted — and fit s300-sim as they fit the s150-* examples. Their format, and how to write your own, is in The Market Model; the AI Traders & News exercises use them in class.

    Watching the news¶

    Where What
    terminal-gui, news view Every headline as it is published, with status and sentiment
    docker exec edumatcher-sim pm-news list Headlines printed as they arrive, until Ctrl+C
    GET /api/v1/news (any API key) Today's headlines, and the sector of every symbol

    Try it¶

    1. Pick a quiet symbol in book-gui and note its price and the value (pm-market-sim --status --id OPS01 lists it if it is far off; the admin API, GET /api/v1/admin/sim with the OPS01 key, gives all of them).
    2. Inject a confirmed headline with impact 0.05 and watch the price for a minute. Who traded first? (The tape and the order book show AI ids.)
    3. Inject a rumour with sentiment 0.8, credibility 0.9, and no impact worth speaking of (--impact 0.001). Does the price move? Why not much?
    4. Retract it. Then try again without the market maker (Tuning the Traders shows how to remove it).
