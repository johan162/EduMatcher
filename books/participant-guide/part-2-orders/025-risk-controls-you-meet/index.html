    Risk Controls You Meet¶

    Learning objectives

    After reading this page you will understand:

    • Which exchange safeguards can refuse, pause or cancel your orders
    • What each rejection or cancellation looks like, and what to do about it
    • What happens to your resting orders when a symbol halts, and how it reopens
    • What happens to your orders when you use the kill switch or your session ends
    • Where to look up the limits that apply to a symbol

    Prerequisites: Order Types and A Full Trading Day.

    Every exchange protects its market against mistakes and disorder: a mistyped price, an extra zero on a quantity, a price that runs away in seconds, a bot that will not stop. EduMatcher has the same safeguards. The operator sets them in the exchange configuration; this page explains what they do to your orders. How they are configured is in the Operator's Guide chapter Risk Controls.

    Control Acts on You see
    Tick size Every price you type The order is refused before it is sent
    Order size and value limits A new or amended order REJECTED MAX_ORDER_QTY / MAX_ORDER_VALUE
    Price collars A new or amended priced order REJECTED COLLAR_BREACH
    Circuit breakers The symbol, after a sharp price move The symbol halts; MARKET/IOC/FOK rejected CIRCUIT_BREAKER_ACTIVE
    Operator halts A symbol or the whole market MARKET/IOC/FOK rejected INSTRUMENT_HALTED
    Session phase Every order REJECTED MARKET_CLOSED / SESSION_NOT_PERMITTED
    Kill switch Your resting orders CANCELLED … reason=KILL_SWITCH
    Disconnect behaviour Your resting orders when you log off Orders cancelled, or left resting
    Self-match prevention Your order meeting your own resting order CANCELLED … reason=SELF_MATCH_PREVENTED
    Rate limits How fast a program sends Requests refused for a moment

    The messages below are what the trader console (pm-alf-console) prints; the order id is shortened here. Other front-ends show the same code and reason text.

    Tick size¶

    Every symbol has a smallest price step, its tick: 0.01 for a symbol with two decimals. A price that is not a whole number of ticks is refused by the console before anything is sent:

    [TRADER01]> NEW|SYM=AAPL|SIDE=BUY|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=100|PRICE=150.001
150.001 is not a multiple of AAPL's tick size 0.01

    SYMBOLS lists every symbol's tick. The ALF and REST gateways refuse such a price with TICK_VIOLATION.

    Order size and value limits¶

    A symbol can cap the size of a single order (max_order_qty) and its value, quantity × price (max_order_value). They catch the order with an extra zero that every price check would let through.

    REJECTED  601cb911… code=MAX_ORDER_QTY  Quantity 2000 exceeds max_order_qty 1000
REJECTED  50745acf… code=MAX_ORDER_VALUE  Order value 60000.00 exceeds max_order_value 50000.0
    • The quantity cap applies to every order type; the value cap only to orders that carry a price (not MARKET).
    • An amend is checked against the same caps, so you cannot place a small order and amend it up.
    • An order over a cap is rejected, never cut down to fit. Split it into several orders, each within the limits.
    • A symbol with no caps configured has no limit.

    Price collars¶

    A collar rejects an order whose price is too far from where the symbol trades, the classic "fat finger". It has two bands, both a percentage:

    Band Measured from Typical width
    Static The symbol's reference price at the start of the day Wide, e.g. ±20%
    Dynamic The last trade of the session (skipped until the first trade) Narrow, e.g. ±2% 
    REJECTED  cd464bb5… code=COLLAR_BREACH  STATIC_COLLAR_BREACH: price 13000 ticks is outside [8000, 12000] ticks (±20% from reference 10000)
REJECTED  ea225ff3… code=COLLAR_BREACH  DYNAMIC_COLLAR_BREACH: price 11100 ticks is outside [9000, 11000] ticks (±10% from last trade 10000)

    The band is printed in ticks: [9000, 11000] on a two-decimal symbol is 90.00 to 110.00.

    • Every order with a price is checked: LIMIT, ICEBERG, IOC, FOK, STOP_LIMIT, an amend's new price, and each leg of an OCO or combo (one leg outside the band rejects the whole OCO or combo).
    • A MARKET order has no price and is not collared — one more reason to prefer a priced IOC when the book is thin.
    • A market maker's two-sided quote is not collared.
    • The dynamic band moves with every trade. A price refused now may be accepted a few trades later, once the market has moved towards it.

    Circuit breakers and halts¶

    A collar refuses one order. A circuit breaker watches the trades instead, and when a trade moves the price too far from its recent average (for example more than 5% within five minutes), it halts the symbol. The trade that tripped the breaker stands; trading in that symbol then pauses.

    While a symbol is halted:

    Your order What happens
    MARKET, IOC, FOK Rejected: CIRCUIT_BREAKER_ACTIVE AAPL is halted — MARKET orders rejected during circuit breaker halt
    LIMIT, ICEBERG Accepted and rests, without matching, until the reopening
    STOP, STOP_LIMIT, TRAILING_STOP Accepted and stored; they can trigger only on a trade
    Orders already resting Stay on the book, untouched
    Market-maker quotes Cancelled when the halt starts; new quotes are rejected
    Amend and cancel Work as usual

    The halt lasts as long as its configured level says — a breaker can have several levels, a bigger move halting for longer. A short random delay is added to its end, so nobody can time an order to arrive at the last instant.

    The reopening is an auction. Because limit orders rest without matching during the halt, buyers and sellers can end up crossing each other. At the end of the halt the engine uncrosses the book at a single price, exactly like the opening auction, and then continuous trading resumes. If that reopening price would be too far from the price before the halt, the symbol does not reopen yet: the allowed band widens and the halt is extended, step by step, until a reopening price fits. Your orders keep resting throughout. Auction prices never trip the breaker themselves.

    Use the halt

    A halt is time to think, not just a pause. Check the news, look at the book, and cancel or re-price resting orders you no longer want filled at the reopening. A limit order placed during the halt takes part in the reopening auction.

    The trader console does not announce a halt; you learn of it from the CIRCUIT_BREAKER_ACTIVE rejection. The Trading GUI and TapeDeck show a HALT badge on the symbol, with the level and the expected reopening time.

    Operator halts¶

    The operator can halt a single symbol or the whole market at any time — for pending news, a technical problem, or a classroom exercise. While halted, orders behave exactly as under a circuit breaker, but the rejection code is different:

    REJECTED  0b87dfbd… code=INSTRUMENT_HALTED  AAPL is halted — FOK orders rejected during trading halt

    The difference matters: a CIRCUIT_BREAKER_ACTIVE halt ends by itself; an INSTRUMENT_HALTED halt, without a level, lasts until the operator lifts it.

    Session phase¶

    Outside continuous trading the phase decides what you may send; see A Full Trading Day.

    Rejection Cause
    MARKET_CLOSED Market is closed The session is CLOSED; no order is accepted
    SESSION_NOT_PERMITTED MARKET orders not accepted during PRE_OPEN A MARKET, IOC or FOK outside continuous trading
    SESSION_NOT_PERMITTED ATO orders only accepted during opening auction TIF=ATO outside the opening auction (likewise ATC)

    Type SESSION in the trader console to see the current phase.

    The kill switch¶

    KILL cancels all your resting orders and quotes at once; KILL|SYM=AAPL only those on one symbol. It is your emergency brake when a strategy or a bot misbehaves:

    [TRADER01]> KILL
[10:12:48.453] CANCELLED 4d11d22b… reason=KILL_SWITCH
[10:12:48.455] CANCELLED 8ec835fc… reason=KILL_SWITCH
[10:12:48.456] KILL ACK  orders=2 quote_legs=0

    The kill switch does not stop you: you can send new orders right after it. Fills that already happened are not undone.

    An operator can use the same switch on your gateway, on a symbol, or on the whole market; your orders are then cancelled with the same KILL_SWITCH reason. An operator can also disconnect your session, which cancels your orders as described next.

    When your session ends¶

    When you log off — EXIT, closing the window, a crash or a dropped network connection — the exchange applies your participant's disconnect behaviour, set by the operator:

    Setting Your resting orders Your quotes
    CANCEL_QUOTES_ONLY (the default) Stay on the book Cancelled
    CANCEL_ALL Cancelled Cancelled
    LEAVE_ALL Stay on the book Stay on the book

    Ask your instructor which applies to you. Under CANCEL_QUOTES_ONLY or LEAVE_ALL an order you forgot can still fill while you are away; when you log on again, ORDERS shows what is still resting. Your orders also stay on the book across a restart of the exchange itself — GTC orders indefinitely, DAY orders until the end of their trading day.

    Self-match prevention¶

    If your order would trade against your own resting order, the exchange can stop it. What happens depends on the SMP= value of the incoming order, or, when you leave it out, your participant's configured default:

    Value Effect
    NONE The trade happens: you trade with yourself
    CANCEL_AGGRESSOR Your incoming order is cancelled; the resting one stays
    CANCEL_RESTING Your resting order is cancelled; the incoming order trades on against others
    CANCEL_BOTH Both are cancelled

    A cancelled order shows reason=SELF_MATCH_PREVENTED. It is a cancellation, not a rejection: the order may already have traded with other participants before it met your own. See the FAQ.

    Rate limits¶

    The gateways that programs use limit how fast one session may send:

    • REST API (pm-api-gwy): a number of write requests (orders, amends, cancels) per second, with a short burst allowance; above it requests are refused with HTTP 429 RATE_LIMIT. Slow down and resend.
    • ALF TCP gateway (pm-alf-gwy): commands above the per-session rate are refused with RATE_LIMITED. A session that keeps sending commands the gateway refuses (by default 50 errors within a minute) is disconnected.

    A person typing in the trader console never comes near these limits; a bot in a tight loop does.

    Looking up a symbol's limits¶

    • SYMBOLS in the trader console shows each symbol's tick size and, for market makers, the quoting obligation.
    • The REST call GET /api/v1/reference/symbols returns each symbol's tick size, collar bands, order limits and circuit-breaker levels — see the REST reference.
    • Your instructor can show the full risk configuration, which an ADMIN sees on the Trading GUI's Risk Controls screen.

    Reject codes at a glance¶

    Code Meaning What to do
    UNKNOWN_SYMBOL The symbol is not listed Run SYMBOLS
    MISSING_FIELD / UNSUPPORTED_FIELD / INVALID_VALUE A field the order type needs is missing, not allowed, or malformed See Fields each type takes
    TICK_VIOLATION Price not on the tick grid Round to the tick
    MAX_ORDER_QTY / MAX_ORDER_VALUE Order too large Split it
    COLLAR_BREACH Price outside the collar Price closer to the market
    CIRCUIT_BREAKER_ACTIVE Symbol halted by a circuit breaker Wait for the reopening, or rest a LIMIT
    INSTRUMENT_HALTED Symbol halted by the operator Wait for the operator, or rest a LIMIT
    MARKET_CLOSED Session closed Wait for the next session
    SESSION_NOT_PERMITTED Not allowed in this phase Use a LIMIT, or the right TIF
    INSUFFICIENT_LIQUIDITY A FOK (or a held-back combo) could not fill in full Reduce the size or widen the price
    ORDER_NOT_FOUND Amend or cancel of an order that is not resting Check ORDERS; it may have filled
    AMEND_NOT_PERMITTED That order type cannot be amended Cancel and re-enter
    QTY_OUT_OF_RANGE / PRICE_OUT_OF_RANGE Amend to an impossible quantity or price Check the new values
    DUPLICATE_ORDER The same order id sent twice (a program resending an order) Give each order a new id
    RATE_LIMITED Sending too fast Slow down

    The message reference lists every code.
