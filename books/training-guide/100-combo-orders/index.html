    Combo Orders¶

    Objective¶

    Build and close multi-leg strategies, size their hedges, and distinguish entry checks from lifetime guarantees. Use OCO (One-Cancels-Other) for alternative entries and exits, then test partial fills, stops, failure cascades, session boundaries and recovery.

    Background reading

    • Combo Orders
    • OCO

    Prerequisites¶

    • Chapters 01–09 completed.
    • Two trader gateways connected so you can stage opposing liquidity.
    • An admin console for clearing the training books.
    • For the stop-trigger labs, a third configured and connected trader, TRADER03, to print a trigger trade without changing TRADER01's position. Add it using the gateway configuration procedure from Configuring Startup, then connect with pm-alf-console --id TRADER03.
    • For the three-leg basket, TSLA configured alongside AAPL and MSFT.

    Lab discipline¶

    Use an isolated classroom exchange, not a shared trading session. Stop market-maker bots and AI traders. Keep the session in CONTINUOUS with the scheduler stopped, using the freeze/advance procedure. The session exercise explicitly changes this.

    Exercises 1–4 build on each other. Before Exercise 5 and before each independent run or variant in Exercises 6–15, clear the affected books:

    [GW_ADMIN|ADMIN]> CANCEL_SYM|SYM=AAPL
[GW_ADMIN|ADMIN]> CANCEL_SYM|SYM=MSFT
[GW_ADMIN|ADMIN]> CANCEL_SYM|SYM=TSLA

    Only use the TSLA command if that symbol is configured. These commands cancel orders, not positions or trade history. Record positions before and after each lab and measure changes, rather than assuming a flat account. Price collars and circuit breakers must allow the example prices (AAPL 148–153, MSFT near 420 and TSLA near 250); have the instructor align the configuration before starting rather than treating a risk rejection as a strategy result.

    Copy commands without the console prompt. Replace <...> placeholders with actual IDs. Use a fresh COMBO_ID or OCO_ID on every repeat. Save the child IDs from acknowledgements: ORDERS shows working orders, not a history of filled orders or a parent-combo status table. Use fill/cancel messages and parent status messages as evidence; pm-audit --terminal is useful alongside the consoles.

    Progression¶

    Exercises Skill
    1–4 Entry atomicity, resting leg risk and parent cancellation
    5–8 OCO entries/exits, partial fills, breakouts and trailing protection
    9–10 Hedge ratios, closing a pair and three-to-ten-leg baskets
    11–12 Depth, execution instructions and conditional combo legs
    13–15 Failure handling, self-match prevention, sessions and recovery

    Background¶

    A combo order bundles two to ten legs on distinct symbols, each with its own side and quantity, and submits them as one message with a shared COMBO_ID.

    COMBO_TYPE=AON (all-or-none) gives you an atomicity guarantee — but it is narrower than it first sounds, and the exact shape of it is the main thing this chapter teaches:

    The experiments below begin with ordinary limit legs, external liquidity, continuous matching and no halt. AON is a submission-time liquidity check, not transaction rollback if a later child encounters SMP or another execution restriction; Exercises 12–14 explore those boundaries.

    AON is atomic at entry, not for the combo's lifetime

    When you submit an AON combo the engine checks, at that instant, whether every leg can fill completely. In the controlled setup above, there are three outcomes:

    • All legs can fill → all legs execute together. No partial.
    • A leg is short, and another would trade at once → the combo is rejected: its first leg with INSUFFICIENT_LIQUIDITY, and the combo FAILED. Resting the leg that would trade would leave its book crossed.
    • A leg is short, and no leg would trade → no leg matches at all. Every leg is posted to its book as an ordinary resting order.

    That third outcome is where the nuance lives. Those resting legs are now normal orders. Nothing stops another participant from hitting one of them later, and when that happens the combo moves to PARTIALLY_MATCHED — one leg filled, the others still resting. You have leg risk again.

    In this controlled all-limit setup, a depth shortfall cannot make the combo execute only part of its intended entry. It can still end up half-filled while it rests. Exercises 2 and 3 show both halves of that.

    The combo's lifecycle states are PENDING, PARTIALLY_MATCHED, MATCHED, FAILED (a leg was cancelled, rejected or expired, so the siblings cascade-cancel), CANCELLED and REJECTED.

    Exercise 1: Simple Two-Leg Combo¶

    To see the combo fill atomically, first guarantee liquidity on both legs with explicit counter-orders, and nothing else. Market-maker quotes from earlier chapters would get in the way — TRADER02's MSFT buy at 420.50, for example, would trade straight away with a market maker's cheaper ask instead of resting. So stop any pm-mm-bot, clear both symbols from the operator console, and then place the counter-orders:

    [GW_ADMIN|ADMIN]> CANCEL_SYM|SYM=AAPL
[GW_ADMIN|ADMIN]> CANCEL_SYM|SYM=MSFT

    [TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=AAPL|SIDE=SELL|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=100|PRICE=150.10|TIF=DAY
[TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=MSFT|SIDE=BUY|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=50|PRICE=420.50|TIF=DAY

    Now submit the combo — buy AAPL and sell MSFT atomically:

    [TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=COMBO|COMBO_ID=PAIR-001|COMBO_TYPE=AON|TIF=DAY|LEG_COUNT=2|LEG0.SYM=AAPL|LEG0.SIDE=BUY|LEG0.QTY=100|LEG0.PRICE=150.10|LEG1.SYM=MSFT|LEG1.SIDE=SELL|LEG1.QTY=50|LEG1.PRICE=420.50

    Because both counter-orders above match the combo's leg prices exactly, the engine can fill both legs simultaneously.

    Checkpoint: the trader console shows a fill on each leg followed by COMBO MATCHED PAIR-001, and BOOK|SYM=AAPL / BOOK|SYM=MSFT in the operator console show both counter-orders gone.

    Observation: when the pre-check passes, both legs execute in the same pass — this is the case the AON guarantee is designed for.

    Exercise 2: Atomicity at Entry — One Short Leg Blocks Every Leg¶

    Now check the pre-check: when liquidity is missing on one leg, no leg matches, even the one that could have — and the combo is rejected:

    1. Exercise 1 used up the AAPL sell, so place a new one: [TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=AAPL|SIDE=SELL|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=100|PRICE=150.10|TIF=DAY.
    2. Do not place any MSFT buy at 420.50 — cancel or avoid resting MSFT liquidity at that price so the second leg has nothing to match against.
    3. Submit a new combo with a fresh ID:
    [TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=COMBO|COMBO_ID=PAIR-002|COMBO_TYPE=AON|TIF=DAY|LEG_COUNT=2|LEG0.SYM=AAPL|LEG0.SIDE=BUY|LEG0.QTY=100|LEG0.PRICE=150.10|LEG1.SYM=MSFT|LEG1.SIDE=SELL|LEG1.QTY=50|LEG1.PRICE=420.50
    1. The AAPL leg is rejected with INSUFFICIENT_LIQUIDITY and the combo FAILED; the MSFT leg is never posted. Check BOOK|SYM=AAPL in the operator console and confirm the AAPL sell at 150.10 is still resting, unfilled, proving the combo did not execute the AAPL leg alone even though a matching counter-order existed for it.

    Checkpoint: the combo did not execute the AAPL leg even though a matching counter-order was sitting there, and nothing of the combo rests.

    Operational rationale: without the entry pre-check you would pick up accidental directional inventory the moment you submitted an unbalanced combo. And the AAPL leg could not rest instead: a buy at 150.10 resting against a sell at 150.10 would leave the book crossed.

    Exercise 3: Leg Risk Returns Once the Combo Rests¶

    A combo with no leg that can trade yet rests with both legs in their books. This is the case people are surprised by, so cause it deliberately.

    1. Submit a combo whose AAPL leg bids below TRADER02's AAPL sell at 150.10, and confirm it is resting and PENDING:

    [TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=COMBO|COMBO_ID=PAIR-003|COMBO_TYPE=AON|TIF=DAY|LEG_COUNT=2|LEG0.SYM=AAPL|LEG0.SIDE=BUY|LEG0.QTY=100|LEG0.PRICE=150.00|LEG1.SYM=MSFT|LEG1.SIDE=SELL|LEG1.QTY=50|LEG1.PRICE=420.50
[TRADER01]> ORDERS

    You should see both child legs — the AAPL buy at 150.00 and the MSFT sell at 420.50 — with status NEW.

    2. From TRADER02, hit only the MSFT leg:

    [TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=MSFT|SIDE=BUY|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=50|PRICE=420.50|TIF=DAY

    3. Look at what happened to the combo:

    [TRADER01]> ORDERS

    The MSFT fill message says FILLED; that leg is no longer in the working ORDERS list. The AAPL leg is still NEW, resting. The combo has moved to PARTIALLY_MATCHED.

    TRADER01 is now short 50 MSFT with no offsetting AAPL position — precisely the leg risk the combo was supposed to prevent. The AON guarantee was never violated: it applied at submission, and at submission nothing executed.

    Checkpoint: the combo is PARTIALLY_MATCHED with one leg filled and one resting, and you can explain why this does not contradict "all-or-none".

    What a real desk does about this

    Because a resting AON combo can be picked apart, desks do not leave them resting. They either submit only when both sides are already fillable, or they cancel the whole combo the moment it fails to execute at entry:

    [TRADER01]> CANCEL|COMBO_ID=PAIR-003

    That cancels every remaining leg. Fills that already happened are not reversed — cancel the combo before someone hits a leg, not after.

    Clean up before continuing:

    [TRADER01]> CANCEL|COMBO_ID=PAIR-003

    Exercise 4: Cancel a Resting Combo¶

    Exercise 3's clean-up already cancelled PAIR-003. Submit one more combo that cannot fill — there is no MSFT bid at 420.50 now, and the AAPL leg bids below the AAPL sell — so it rests, then cancel it as a whole:

    [TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=COMBO|COMBO_ID=PAIR-004|COMBO_TYPE=AON|TIF=DAY|LEG_COUNT=2|LEG0.SYM=AAPL|LEG0.SIDE=BUY|LEG0.QTY=100|LEG0.PRICE=149.00|LEG1.SYM=MSFT|LEG1.SIDE=SELL|LEG1.QTY=50|LEG1.PRICE=420.50
[TRADER01]> CANCEL|COMBO_ID=PAIR-004

    All legs are cancelled together, and the console reports COMBO CANCELLED PAIR-004.

    Checkpoint: full combo cancellation confirmed.

    Exercise 5: OCO — One-Cancels-Other¶

    Link two independent orders so that when one fully fills or otherwise ends (cancel, rejection or expiry), the other is automatically cancelled. A partial fill alone does not end the pair; Exercise 7 tests this distinction.

    [TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=OCO|OCO_ID=OCO-AAPL-ENTRY|SYM=AAPL|QTY=100|TIF=DAY|LEG1_SIDE=BUY|LEG1_TYPE=LIMIT|LEG1_PRICE=149.50|LEG2_SIDE=BUY|LEG2_TYPE=LIMIT|LEG2_PRICE=148.00

    Make the first leg fill by selling into it from TRADER02:

    [TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=AAPL|SIDE=SELL|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=100|PRICE=149.50|TIF=DAY

    The 149.50 leg fills, and the console reports the sibling at 148.00 cancelled: OCO CANCEL OCO-AAPL-ENTRY sibling=… OCO sibling … reached FILLED. (If the AAPL book still holds asks at or below 149.50 from earlier exercises, the OCO's first leg fills on entry instead — clear AAPL with CANCEL_SYM first for a clean run.)

    To pull an OCO pair yourself before either leg fills, cancel it by its OCO_ID rather than cancelling each leg individually:

    [TRADER01]> CANCEL|OCO_ID=OCO-AAPL-ENTRY

    Checkpoint: filling one OCO leg cancels the other.

    Exercise 6: OCO — Protect an Existing Long Position¶

    A common exit pattern is a take-profit plus a stop-loss. Both legs are SELL, not different sides: each is an alternative way to close the same long position. First acquire 100 shares on the cleared AAPL book:

    [TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=AAPL|SIDE=SELL|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=100|PRICE=150.00|TIF=DAY
[TRADER01]> NEW|SYM=AAPL|SIDE=BUY|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=100|PRICE=150.00|TIF=DAY

    Now submit the exit pair:

    [TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=OCO|OCO_ID=BRACKET-AAPL-001|SYM=AAPL|QTY=100|TIF=DAY|LEG1_SIDE=SELL|LEG1_TYPE=LIMIT|LEG1_PRICE=151.00|LEG2_SIDE=SELL|LEG2_TYPE=STOP|LEG2_STOP=149.00

    Run both branches separately, acquiring a fresh 100-share position and using a fresh OCO ID on the second run:

    • Take profit: TRADER02 buys 100 AAPL at 151.00. The resting limit sells all 100; the stop is cancelled. Gross profit is 100.
    • Stop loss: TRADER02 posts a bid for 101 AAPL at 149.00, then TRADER03 sells 1 at 149.00. That print triggers the stop, whose 100-share market sell consumes the remaining bid; the take-profit is cancelled. Gross loss is 100.

    Quotes merely moving are not enough: these stop exercises create an actual trade at the trigger price.

    This is not an attached, reduce-only bracket

    The OCO does not create or reserve the long position, wait for a separate entry order, or resize itself after partial fills. A stop becomes a market order: 149.00 is a trigger, not a guaranteed execution price. If liquidity is absent, it may execute partly or not at all. You must reconcile the resulting position yourself.

    Checkpoint: demonstrate both exits, identify the sibling cancellation, and calculate P&L from actual fills rather than from the stop trigger.

    Exercise 7: OCO — A Partial Fill Is Not a Completed Choice¶

    Predict first: after 40 shares of a 100-share leg fill, does the sibling remain at 100, resize to 60, or disappear?

    On a cleared AAPL book:

    [TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=OCO|OCO_ID=OCO-PARTIAL-01|SYM=AAPL|QTY=100|TIF=DAY|LEG1_SIDE=BUY|LEG1_TYPE=LIMIT|LEG1_PRICE=149.50|LEG2_SIDE=BUY|LEG2_TYPE=LIMIT|LEG2_PRICE=148.00
[TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=AAPL|SIDE=SELL|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=40|PRICE=149.50|TIF=DAY
[TRADER01]> ORDERS

    The first leg has 60 remaining and is PARTIAL. The sibling still bids for 100, not 60. OCO has not cancelled or resized it.

    1. Sell another 60 at 149.50 from TRADER02. The first leg becomes FILLED and the 148.00 sibling is cancelled.
    2. Reset and repeat with a fresh ID, but after the first 40-share fill, cancel the partially filled child with CANCEL|ID=<child_id>. Both remaining interests disappear. The 40 bought shares remain.
    3. Reset and repeat once more, replacing the child cancel with CANCEL|OCO_ID=<new_oco_id>. Compare the position change, not just the cancellation messages.

    Explain why the higher of two ordinary buy limits usually fills first. These are not two independent opportunities to buy 100 shares each. Nor does the link impose a shared cumulative-fill budget while both legs remain nonterminal. For a partially filled exit OCO, you must manage the remaining position and outstanding quantities yourself.

    Checkpoint: record 40 filled, 60 remaining on one leg and 100 on its sibling; prove that cancellation does not undo the 40-share fill.

    Exercise 8: OCO — Breakout Entry and Protective-Exit Variants¶

    A. Choose a direction only after a breakout¶

    Unlike Exercise 6, this pair has opposite sides. It expresses an alternative entry, not protection of an existing position.

    First establish a last trade of 150.00 with a one-share sell from TRADER02 and a one-share buy from TRADER03. Then submit:

    [TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=OCO|OCO_ID=BREAKOUT-01|SYM=AAPL|QTY=100|TIF=DAY|LEG1_SIDE=BUY|LEG1_TYPE=STOP|LEG1_STOP=151.00|LEG2_SIDE=SELL|LEG2_TYPE=STOP|LEG2_STOP=149.00
    • Up-break run: TRADER02 offers 101 at 151.00; TRADER03 buys 1 at 151.00. The buy stop consumes the other 100 and cancels the sell stop. TRADER01's position change is +100 AAPL.
    • Down-break run: reset, print 150.00 again and recreate the OCO. TRADER02 bids for 101 at 149.00; TRADER03 sells 1 at 149.00. The sell stop consumes the other 100 and cancels the buy stop. The position change is −100 AAPL.

    Repeat with only 1 share at the trigger price, leaving no liquidity for the stop. Observe that a triggered market order can end unfilled and still end its sibling. OCO cancellation does not prove a position was acquired.

    B. Price protection versus execution risk¶

    Acquire a fresh 100-share long at 150.00, as in Exercise 6. Use this exit:

    [TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=OCO|OCO_ID=EXIT-LIMIT-01|SYM=AAPL|QTY=100|TIF=DAY|LEG1_SIDE=SELL|LEG1_TYPE=LIMIT|LEG1_PRICE=153.00|LEG2_SIDE=SELL|LEG2_TYPE=STOP_LIMIT|LEG2_STOP=149.00|LEG2_PRICE=148.90

    TRADER02 bids for 101 at 148.50 and TRADER03 sells 1 there. The gap print triggers the stop-limit, but its sell limit of 148.90 cannot hit 148.50. It remains working, as does the take-profit. You still own the 100 shares. Cancel the group and compare this with the market-stop outcome.

    C. Trail a profitable position¶

    Acquire another 100 shares at 150.00 and submit:

    [TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=OCO|OCO_ID=EXIT-TRAIL-01|SYM=AAPL|QTY=100|TIF=DAY|LEG1_SIDE=SELL|LEG1_TYPE=LIMIT|LEG1_PRICE=153.00|LEG2_SIDE=SELL|LEG2_TYPE=TRAILING_STOP|LEG2_TRAIL=1.00|LEG2_STOP=149.00
    1. TRADER02 offers 1 at 152.00; TRADER03 buys it. The sell trailing trigger ratchets up to 151.00 (152.00 − 1.00).
    2. TRADER02 bids for 101 at 151.00; TRADER03 sells 1 there. The stop sells 100 at the remaining bid and cancels the 153.00 exit.
    3. Explain why the trigger must not move back down on a price decline.

    The explicit initial STOP removes ambiguity about initialization. Without it, a trailing stop needs an existing last trade from which to derive its trigger.

    Checkpoint: distinguish entry versus exit OCO, stop-market versus stop-limit, and fixed versus trailing triggers. For each run, report actual fills, remaining interest and position change.

    Exercise 9: Size a Pairs Trade and Close It¶

    Exercise 1's 100 AAPL versus 50 MSFT is not dollar-neutral: at 150 and 420, the notionals are 15,000 versus 21,000. A combo supplies execution mechanics, not the hedge ratio or a profitable trading signal.

    Choose a gross notional of 42,000 on each side. That requires buying 280 AAPL at 150 and selling 100 MSFT at 420:

    [TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=AAPL|SIDE=SELL|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=280|PRICE=150.00|TIF=DAY
[TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=MSFT|SIDE=BUY|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=100|PRICE=420.00|TIF=DAY
[TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=COMBO|COMBO_ID=NEUTRAL-IN-01|COMBO_TYPE=AON|TIF=DAY|TAG=PAIR-LAB|LEG_COUNT=2|LEG0.SYM=AAPL|LEG0.SIDE=BUY|LEG0.QTY=280|LEG0.PRICE=150.00|LEG1.SYM=MSFT|LEG1.SIDE=SELL|LEG1.QTY=100|LEG1.PRICE=420.00

    After both fill, close the actual filled quantities with a new, opposite-side combo:

    [TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=AAPL|SIDE=BUY|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=280|PRICE=152.00|TIF=DAY
[TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=MSFT|SIDE=SELL|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=100|PRICE=422.00|TIF=DAY
[TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=COMBO|COMBO_ID=NEUTRAL-OUT-01|COMBO_TYPE=AON|TIF=DAY|TAG=PAIR-LAB|LEG_COUNT=2|LEG0.SYM=AAPL|LEG0.SIDE=SELL|LEG0.QTY=280|LEG0.PRICE=152.00|LEG1.SYM=MSFT|LEG1.SIDE=BUY|LEG1.QTY=100|LEG1.PRICE=422.00

    Calculate before reading the answer:

    • Long AAPL: 280 × (152 − 150) = +560.
    • Short MSFT: 100 × (420 − 422) = −200.
    • Gross strategy P&L: +360, before fees. The combined position change over entry and exit is zero.

    Equal notionals are not equal market sensitivities. Discuss how different volatility, beta and correlation could invalidate this hedge. Prices moving together do not guarantee a profit, and a pairs trade is not risk-free arbitrage.

    TAG is a client correlation label; it does not link the two combo parents or make the exit automatic. Cancelling an already filled entry cannot close the position.

    Checkpoint: justify the 280:100 ratio, prove both parents matched, and reconcile four leg fills to +360 gross P&L.

    Exercise 10: Three-Leg Basket, Then Scale the Process¶

    Model a hypothetical relative-value signal: buy AAPL against a basket of MSFT and TSLA. Use unequal quantities to balance cash notionals: 100 × 150 = 25 × 420 + 18 × 250 = 15,000.

    [TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=AAPL|SIDE=SELL|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=100|PRICE=150.00|TIF=DAY
[TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=MSFT|SIDE=BUY|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=25|PRICE=420.00|TIF=DAY
[TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=TSLA|SIDE=BUY|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=18|PRICE=250.00|TIF=DAY
[TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=COMBO|COMBO_ID=BASKET-01|COMBO_TYPE=AON|TIF=DAY|LEG_COUNT=3|LEG0.SYM=AAPL|LEG0.SIDE=BUY|LEG0.QTY=100|LEG0.PRICE=150.00|LEG1.SYM=MSFT|LEG1.SIDE=SELL|LEG1.QTY=25|LEG1.PRICE=420.00|LEG2.SYM=TSLA|LEG2.SIDE=SELL|LEG2.QTY=18|LEG2.PRICE=250.00
    1. Verify all three fills and MATCHED.
    2. Reset and repeat, but stage only 17 TSLA, not 18. Predict the result before submitting. No leg may trade at entry in this controlled all-limit setup; the parent becomes FAILED. All counter-orders remain.
    3. Reset and submit the same basket to empty books. It rests. Fill only 10 AAPL from TRADER02: the parent becomes PARTIALLY_MATCHED, even though no entire leg has finished. Record the remaining quantities. Cancel the parent and identify the residual +10 AAPL position.

    Instructor extension — up to ten legs: configure seven more distinct symbols with known tick sizes and approved price ranges. Have students build a leg worksheet (symbol, side, quantity, limit, notional, available depth), then expand the all-limit basket to LEG_COUNT=10 and LEG0 through LEG9. Run it once with full liquidity and once with one missing share on the last leg. Also try LEG_COUNT=11: the gateway must refuse it. A leg per symbol is mandatory; repeating a symbol is not a way to construct an options butterfly or a two-sided quote.

    As a strategy variation, design a sector rotation basket with several buys and sells. A calendar-spread analogy requires two separately configured symbols; this lab does not add futures expiry, options payoffs or contract multipliers to the exchange.

    Checkpoint: submit a three-leg basket, diagnose a one-share shortfall, and explain how checking each book scales to the supported two-to-ten-leg range.

    Exercise 11: Execution Cost — Depth, MARKET, IOC and FOK Legs¶

    An AON pre-check needs the whole quantity, not merely a visible best price. On cleared books stage this ladder:

    [TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=AAPL|SIDE=SELL|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=40|PRICE=150.00|TIF=DAY
[TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=AAPL|SIDE=SELL|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=60|PRICE=150.10|TIF=DAY
[TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=MSFT|SIDE=BUY|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=20|PRICE=420.50|TIF=DAY
[TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=MSFT|SIDE=BUY|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=30|PRICE=420.40|TIF=DAY
[TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=COMBO|COMBO_ID=DEPTH-01|COMBO_TYPE=AON|TIF=DAY|LEG_COUNT=2|LEG0.SYM=AAPL|LEG0.SIDE=BUY|LEG0.QTY=100|LEG0.TYPE=LIMIT|LEG0.PRICE=150.10|LEG1.SYM=MSFT|LEG1.SIDE=SELL|LEG1.QTY=50|LEG1.TYPE=LIMIT|LEG1.PRICE=420.40

    Calculate the leg VWAPs from executions: AAPL 150.06, MSFT 420.44. The entry receives 21,022 − 15,006 = 6,016 cash before fees, but it also creates a short liability. That receipt is not a profit.

    Repeat with a fresh ID and restaged ladder for each variant:

    Variant Command changes Prediction in this controlled setup
    Price limit too tight AAPL LEG0.PRICE=150.00 Only 40 eligible AAPL; no leg trades; parent FAILED
    One share short Stage 59 instead of 60 at 150.10 Only 99 AAPL; no leg trades; parent FAILED
    Market legs Both .TYPE=MARKET; remove both .PRICE fields Full depth is available; both fill, but no leg price cap
    IOC legs Both .TYPE=IOC; keep both prices Both fill completely when the pre-check passes
    FOK legs Both .TYPE=FOK; keep both prices Both fill completely when the pre-check passes

    Repeat the short-liquidity run with IOC and FOK legs too. Do not import the single-order IOC lesson ("take 99 and cancel 1") into an AON combo: the parent pre-check blocks execution here. Finally, submit each immediate type to empty books; unlike passive limits, those legs cannot wait, so the combo fails instead of remaining PENDING.

    There is no parent net-price limit. Choose each leg's cap yourself and compute the resulting worst-case cash flow. COMBO_TYPE currently supports only AON, not a ratio-fill, net-debit or net-credit execution mode.

    Checkpoint: reconcile quantities and VWAPs across multiple price levels, and explain the interaction between parent AON and each child's execution instruction.

    Exercise 12: Conditional Combo Legs Are Not a Synchronized Trigger¶

    The engine accepts STOP and STOP_LIMIT combo legs, but their triggers belong to their individual symbol books. Use them to study conditional exposure, not as a promise of an atomic future hedge.

    Establish a one-share AAPL trade at 150.00 between TRADER02 and TRADER03. Leave no other interest, then submit:

    [TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=COMBO|COMBO_ID=CONDITIONAL-01|COMBO_TYPE=AON|TIF=DAY|LEG_COUNT=2|LEG0.SYM=AAPL|LEG0.SIDE=BUY|LEG0.QTY=100|LEG0.TYPE=STOP|LEG0.STOP=151.00|LEG1.SYM=MSFT|LEG1.SIDE=BUY|LEG1.QTY=50|LEG1.TYPE=LIMIT|LEG1.PRICE=420.00
    1. Confirm the parent is pending. The AAPL stop is dormant; the MSFT limit is working. There was no immediate opposing liquidity.
    2. TRADER02 offers 101 AAPL at 151.00; TRADER03 buys 1 at 151.00. The print releases the 100-share buy stop, which fills from the remaining offer. MSFT has not filled. Observe PARTIALLY_MATCHED.
    3. TRADER02 sells 50 MSFT at 420.00. The parent now reaches MATCHED. Explain why this is two separate exposures in time, not an atomic event at the AAPL trigger.

    Stop-limit variant: reset and re-establish AAPL's last trade at 150. Replace leg 0 with LEG0.TYPE=STOP_LIMIT|LEG0.STOP=151.00|LEG0.PRICE=151.05. Trigger with a one-share print at 151.20, leaving an offer there. The released buy limit cannot pay 151.20, so it remains unfilled. Fill the MSFT leg and record the resulting unhedged position; then cancel the combo.

    Do not generalize the all-limit entry experiment

    A stop's trigger and subsequent liquidity are separate from the combo's submission-time depth check. Neither the parent ACK nor AON means that all conditional legs will trigger together, or that a stop will fill. The same caution applies to later auctions, halts and SMP interactions.

    Checkpoint: demonstrate both dormant and triggered legs, and distinguish trigger price, limit price and actual fill price.

    Exercise 13: Parent Cancel, Child Failure and Self-Match Prevention¶

    A. Cancel one child, fail the strategy¶

    Submit PAIR-004's passive limits from Exercise 4 with a fresh ID to empty books. Save the child order IDs. Instead of cancelling the parent, send:

    [TRADER01]> CANCEL|ID=<aapl_child_id>

    The parent becomes FAILED and its MSFT sibling is cancelled. Contrast this with CANCEL|COMBO_ID=..., which produces CANCELLED. Repeat after a 10-share AAPL fill: failure cancels the remaining interest, not that fill.

    B. Sufficient displayed depth can still be your own liquidity¶

    Stage an AAPL offer owned by TRADER01, and an MSFT bid owned by TRADER02:

    [TRADER01]> NEW|SYM=AAPL|SIDE=SELL|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=100|PRICE=150.00|TIF=DAY
[TRADER02]> NEW|SYM=MSFT|SIDE=BUY|TYPE=LIMIT|QTY=50|PRICE=420.00|TIF=DAY
[TRADER01]> NEW|TYPE=COMBO|COMBO_ID=SMP-01|COMBO_TYPE=AON|SMP=CANCEL_AGGRESSOR|TIF=DAY|LEG_COUNT=2|LEG0.SYM=AAPL|LEG0.SIDE=BUY|LEG0.QTY=100|LEG0.PRICE=150.00|LEG1.SYM=MSFT|LEG1.SIDE=SELL|LEG1.QTY=50|LEG1.PRICE=420.00

    The first child meets its owner's offer; SMP cancels that aggressing child. The parent fails, the MSFT child is not posted, and there is no trade.

    Reset and compare SMP=CANCEL_RESTING and SMP=CANCEL_BOTH, recording which own orders survive and whether any later leg is posted. Do not equate an accepted parent or displayed depth with guaranteed execution. With CANCEL_RESTING, a child can remain working after its own opposing order is removed; measure the other leg's fills before deciding how to unwind.

    In ALF the combo-level SMP applies to every child. Omitting it uses the gateway's configured default; SMP=NONE explicitly allows self-matching. Do not use self-trades as evidence of genuine external hedge liquidity. OCO has no per-request SMP field: its legs use the gateway default.

    C. Reprice a child without re-entering the parent¶

    On empty books submit a fresh passive combo buying 100 AAPL at 149.00 and selling 50 MSFT at 420.00. Then TRADER02 offers 100 AAPL at 150.00. Amend the saved AAPL child ID:

    [TRADER01]> AMEND|ID=<aapl_child_id>|PRICE=150.00|RTAG=COMBO-REPRICE-01

    The amended child can fill alone, moving the parent to PARTIALLY_MATCHED. Its sibling remains working. Complete it with a 50-share MSFT buy from TRADER02, or cancel the parent and reconcile the AAPL exposure.

    There is no parent-wide atomic amend command. A child reprice is an ordinary order amendment, not a resubmission of the original AON package. Apply Amending Orders's priority rules, and do not assume the other child limits were changed.

    Checkpoint: explain why the same displayed depth can produce different results under different SMP policies, why a child amend can create leg risk, and why cascade cancellation cannot roll back a previous execution.

    Exercise 14: Validation and TIF — When Should Nothing Be Posted?¶

    A. Reject malformed intent before trading¶

    Starting from a valid passive combo command, change one thing at a time. Use a new label for every attempt and preserve the error message:

    Change What to establish
    LEG_COUNT=1 or 11 Gateway refuses the supported-range violation
    Use AAPL for both legs Engine refuses duplicate symbols
    Remove a required limit .PRICE Gateway refuses the missing field
    Supply a price between that symbol's valid ticks Gateway refuses an off-grid price; each symbol has its own tick scale
    LEG0.TYPE=TRAILING_STOP or ICEBERG Unsupported combo leg shape is refused
    Set a leg quantity to zero Invalid quantity is refused
    Use an unconfigured symbol Submission is refused, not partially posted

    Combo legs support LIMIT, MARKET, IOC, FOK, STOP and STOP_LIMIT. OCO also supports TRAILING_STOP, but not ICEBERG. For OCO, try an immediate MARKET, IOC and FOK first leg against staged sufficient liquidity, paired with a passive second leg: when the first ends at entry, the second must not trade. Repeat IOC with too little liquidity and FOK with none; compare actual fills before sibling cancellation.

    B. DAY/GTC and auction-only interest¶

    With the scheduler stopped and sessions enabled, submit passive combos under two fresh labels, one TIF=DAY and one TIF=GTC, to empty books. Advance from CONTINUOUS through CLOSING_AUCTION to CLOSED. The DAY children expire and the parent fails; GTC interest survives. Cancel the GTC combo after reopening.

    Now use Auctions's manual procedure:

    1. In OPENING_AUCTION, submit an all-limit TIF=ATO combo to empty books. Stage counter-interest on only one symbol, then advance to CONTINUOUS. Record auction fills and expiries of unmatched ATO children, the parent outcome and any residual position.
    2. Repeat in CLOSING_AUCTION with TIF=ATC, then advance to CLOSED.
    3. In a no-matching phase, compare passive LIMIT legs with a combo containing MARKET, IOC or FOK. Those immediate instructions cannot wait for an auction and fail as child orders; passive interest can wait.

    Do not expect a cross-symbol auction to rerun the original combo depth check as a synchronized transaction. Each book uncrosses separately. Distinguish a structural parent rejection from an accepted parent whose child later fails, and distinguish TIF=DAY from LEG0.TYPE=IOC: TIF controls lifetime; the leg type controls execution.

    C. A halted hedge leg¶

    Using Risk Controls's manual halt procedure, halt MSFT on an empty book with HALT_SYM|SYM=MSFT. Submit a fresh passive two-limit combo to empty books; its MSFT child can rest, but must not match while halted. Then, on freshly cleared books, repeat with an MSFT MARKET, IOC or FOK child: that child cannot rest and the parent fails.

    Use the leg rejection to distinguish INSTRUMENT_HALTED from an ordinary liquidity shortfall. Resume with RESUME_SYM|SYM=MSFT and inspect actual fills; do not assume resuming reconstructs an atomic hedge. Restore CONTINUOUS and clear all interest before leaving this lab.

    Checkpoint: separate gateway errors, parent rejection, child failure and expiry, using the ACKs and status events rather than a single "accepted" line.

    Exercise 15: Advanced Operations — Recovery and Strategy Liquidity¶

    This lab extends Persistence & Recovery. Use a dedicated data directory, the same configuration on restart, and a clean stop. Do not hand-edit persistence files to manufacture a result.

    A. Recover the relationship, not just the orders¶

    1. On empty books submit a passive two-limit combo with COMBO_ID=RECOVER-GTC-01 and TIF=GTC. Also submit a passive OCO with TIF=GTC. Record parent labels and all child IDs.
    2. Stop and restart the engine using the same data directory. Reconnect the gateways and restore CONTINUOUS if sessions are enabled.
    3. Cancel the restored combo by COMBO_ID; both working children must disappear, proving the parent-child relationship was restored.
    4. Fill one restored OCO leg completely. Its restored sibling must cancel, proving that restoring two orders also restored their OCO relationship.
    5. Repeat with a partially filled GTC combo, recording filled quantities before restart. Complete its remaining legs afterwards and check the resulting parent status; do not resubmit the original full quantities.

    Comparison: repeat the passive combo with TIF=DAY and restart on the same business day, without a session-close transition first. Individual DAY child orders can survive, but the DAY combo parent is not persisted. Do not assume CANCEL|COMBO_ID=... can manage those restored plain orders; reconcile and cancel them by their child IDs. This is different from the DAY expiry exercise at session close.

    B. Provide liquidity for another participant's strategy¶

    As TRADER02, post the opposite of Exercise 9's entry as a passive live combo: sell 280 AAPL at 150 and buy 100 MSFT at 420. With both legs resting, submit the entry from TRADER01. Both parents can complete through ordinary book matching; there is no separate combo order book.

    Repeat, but have TRADER03 take just part of one resting provider leg first. Recompute the depth before TRADER01 submits. The provider's remaining pair is no longer a guaranteed hedge package. This is why a strategy liquidity provider needs per-leg inventory monitoring.

    Instructor configuration extension: use market_maker_combos to seed passive strategy interest at startup. Verify and deploy the configuration before restarting. Inspect the resulting child orders and compare them with a live-submitted combo. Seeded combos have the internal owner MM, not the live TRADER02 identity; a trader's CANCEL|COMBO_ID=... cannot cancel them. Coordinate cleanup with the admin. Do not treat startup seeding as a promise of entry-time atomic execution.

    Checkpoint: prove restored GTC relationships still work, explain the DAY-parent limitation, and distinguish live combo liquidity from configuration-owned seeds.

    When to Use Combos vs OCO¶

    Use Case Mechanism
    Spread / pairs trade (buy A + sell B) Combo
    Hedging (must have both sides or neither) Combo — but see the caveat below
    Take-profit + stop-loss (only want one to execute) OCO
    Multiple entries at different prices (only want one) OCO

    AON is not a lifetime execution transaction

    The entry liquidity check prevents the ordinary all-limit, short-depth case from executing only one leg. Resting fills, conditional triggers, amendments, auctions and execution restrictions can still leave leg risk. Stage external liquidity, check each leg's eligibility, and reconcile actual fills before cancelling or unwinding. Cancellation never rolls back an execution.

    Reflection¶

    An AON combo is described as "all-or-none", yet a combo can reach a state where one leg is filled and another is still resting. Explain how both statements are true at once, and name the moment at which the guarantee applies.

    Why does a Combo check liquidity at entry while OCO reacts to a child reaching a terminal state? How do partial fills change the risk in each case, and why does the distinction matter for a pairs trade versus a take-profit/stop-loss pair?

    For that comparison, state the assumptions under which the entry liquidity check is sufficient. Would your answer change with SMP, conditional legs or a later child amendment?

    Strategy design challenge¶

    Choose a pairs trade, a three-symbol rotation, or an OCO-protected long. Give another student your strategy and ask them to break its assumptions using one of the failure cases above.

    Submit a short worksheet containing:

    1. The thesis and a losing scenario — not just the expected profit.
    2. Symbols, sides, quantities, tick-valid prices and the hedge-ratio argument.
    3. Entry and exit commands, including who supplies the opposing liquidity.
    4. Predicted ACKs, fills, cancellations and parent states for a successful run, a one-share liquidity shortfall and a partial-fill interruption.
    5. Actual fills and position changes, gross P&L, and a plan for residual exposure if the strategy cannot complete.
    6. What remains live at session close or restart, and who can cancel it.

    Completion standard: the peer must reproduce the successful run and one failure case. A parent ACK, an empty ORDERS list or a sibling cancellation alone is not evidence that the intended strategy executed.
