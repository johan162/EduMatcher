## Notes on selected answers

- **Q1(E):** A bond's coupon rate reflects the issuer's creditworthiness at issuance — riskier issuers pay more, so E is false.
- **Q4(A):** CME uses a largely pro-rata "allocation" algorithm for SOFR futures but FIFO for equity index futures (E-mini S&P 500, E-mini Nasdaq 100), so a blanket "every product" claim is false.
- **Q6(C, D):** A short position's loss is unlimited in theory, because a rising price has no ceiling (D false). Long and short describe the direction of any position, in equities as much as in futures (C false).
- **Q9(A, B, C):** On most venues, market orders cannot rest in the book (B false; some futures venues instead convert the remainder into a protected limit order); a trailing stop is not a resting limit order (C false); its ratchet advances in only one favourable direction and freezes otherwise (A false).
- **Q19(B):** Buying at the bid earns the half-spread, $0.025 × 500 = $12.50; the full spread ($25) is earned only on a complete round trip (a later sale at the ask).
- **Q21(A, B):** The auction's price-improvement rule means a $152 bidder still only pays the $151 equilibrium price; the $152 sell order does not execute because no buyers remain at that price.
- **Q26(D):** Position limits (unit caps) and credit limits (notional/exposure caps) measure different things, despite often being grouped together.
- **Q29(A, E):** Knight Capital's individual fills were priced at real, prevailing market prices and were therefore *not* clearly erroneous in the regulatory sense — the catastrophe was the accumulated volume, not the price of any single trade. Spoofing orders are placed *without* genuine intent to trade, contrary to statement A.
- **Q30(B):** Maker-taker pays the maker (resting order) a rebate and charges the taker (aggressor) a fee — the reverse of what B claims.
