## Notes on selected answers

- **Q2(C, D, E):** SpaceX's 2026 IPO priced below $200/share purely because of its chosen share count relative to a ~$1.77 trillion valuation — it says nothing about company quality (E false). Underwriters rarely end up holding shares themselves; the roadshow process is designed to avoid that (D false). Valuation and share price are linked precisely through the share count the company chooses (C false).
- **Q5(D, E):** A short seller must buy back at whatever the price has become, and a rising price has no ceiling, so the loss is unlimited (E false). A short seller profits when the price *falls* (D false). Check A: (50 − 42) × 100 = $800; C: (61 − 50) × 100 = $1,100 loss.
- **Q7(A, B):** NASDAQ launched in 1971 as an electronic quotation system with no trading floor (B false). Most major exchanges demutualised and are now for-profit companies, several of them listed (A false).
- **Q9(B, E):** A limit price is the *worst* acceptable price, not a target or "best hoped for" price (B false). Unfilled limit orders rest in the book as passive orders rather than being rejected (E false).
- **Q10(A, C):** Market orders are the reverse of what C claims — they favour execution certainty over price certainty. On most venues market orders cannot rest in the book; if unfilled immediately, they are cancelled (A false).
- **Q14(B, E):** A price amendment always loses priority and receives a new timestamp — it is never retained (B false). Under strict FIFO, the earlier-arriving Order X would receive all 60 lots, not Order Y; pro-rata is what splits the fill proportionally (E false/garbled reasoning).
- **Q20(E):** In a call auction, every matched participant trades at the single equilibrium price; a seller who asked $150 when the clearing price is $151 receives $151, not their original ask (E false).
- **Q22(A, D):** The DJIA is price-weighted, not cap-weighted (D false). A free-float methodology specifically excludes closely-held founder/government stakes from the count used in index calculations (A false).
- **Q23(E):** Most headline indexes are price-return indexes: an ordinary dividend is allowed to pull the index level down on the ex-date, and only *special* dividends are absorbed into the divisor. Investors who want dividends counted use the separate total-return version.
- **Q24(C):** A *static* collar compares against the previous close; a *dynamic* collar compares against the most recent trade — C reverses these.
